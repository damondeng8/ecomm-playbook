# Copy: Breakthrough Advertising, Propaganda, and the sales books

Find the desire that already exists, meet the customer at their level of awareness, make one specific claim you can prove, then test it. Before writing anything, fill in or read `docs/copy-brief.md` (template below).

## Rules above every technique

1. **Every claim is true and provable.** Real reviews, real numbers, real deadlines, real stock counts.
2. **No fake urgency or scarcity.** A deadline that resets is a lie.
3. **Persuade people toward something that helps them.** Bernays shows how persuasion works on groups. Use it to make a good product understood, never to mislead.
4. **Health, income, and results claims** need evidence and a disclaimer (`ecomm-stack` (`references/compliance.md`)).

## Breakthrough Advertising (Eugene Schwartz)

**Copy does not create desire. It channels desire that already exists onto your product.** Pick the desire by three measures: how urgent it is, how long it lasts, how many people have it.

### Five levels of awareness: where the headline starts

| The customer… | Start with | Example shape |
|---|---|---|
| **Most aware:** knows the product, wants it | The product and the deal | Name, price, offer |
| **Product aware:** knows it, not sure | Proof, the difference, the offer | "Why 12,000 runners switched" |
| **Solution aware:** wants the result, doesn't know you | The result, then your mechanism | "Sleep cool without a new mattress" |
| **Problem aware:** feels the problem, knows no fix | The problem, named exactly | "Wake up at 3 a.m. soaked?" |
| **Unaware** | A story or identity, not the product | "What night-shift nurses know about sleep" |

Cold ad traffic is mostly problem aware or unaware. Email subscribers and returning visitors are product or most aware. One page rarely serves both. Write for the traffic the screen actually gets.

### Five stages of market sophistication: how to state the claim

| Stage | The market has heard… | Do this |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Nothing like it | State the claim plainly |
| 2 | The claim | Enlarge it, push it to the limit |
| 3 | Every version of the claim | Introduce a **new mechanism**: how it works |
| 4 | Mechanisms too | Elaborate the mechanism: easier, faster, more certain |
| 5 | Everything; it believes none of it | Lead with identity: who the customer becomes |

### Techniques inside the copy

| Technique | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Intensification** | Make the desire vivid: show the result in different scenes, angles, and uses |
| **Identification** | Show the kind of person who owns it |
| **Gradualization** | Start from statements the reader already accepts, lead step by step to the claim |
| **Redefinition** | Reframe a drawback: expensive → costs less per use |
| **Mechanization** | Show exactly how it works, so the claim is believable |
| **Concentration** | Show, fairly, why the alternatives fail |
| **Camouflage** | Borrow the format the reader already trusts (an article, a letter from a person) |

## Propaganda (Edward Bernays)

1. **People decide in groups.** Find the group the customer belongs to and the people it listens to.
2. **Third-party authority beats your own voice.** A dermatologist, a coach, a known customer. Real ones, who really use it.
3. **Change the circumstances, not the pitch.** Bernays' piano example: don't say "buy a piano", make the music room something a good home has. Sell the context the product belongs in.
4. **Make news.** An event, a study, a challenge gives people a reason to talk.
5. **Appeal to the real motive.** People buy for status, belonging, safety, and love, then give a practical reason.

Use: creator and expert partnerships, "as used by" proof, content about the routine or room or identity around the product.

## The other books

| Book | Take |
|---|---|
| **Scientific Advertising** (Hopkins) | Be specific ("removes 94% of film", not "cleans better"). Give the reason why. Offer a sample. Test everything and keep the numbers |
| **Ogilvy on Advertising** | Five times as many people read the headline as the body. Put the promise and the product in it. Long copy sells when it is specific. Research first |
| **Influence** (Cialdini) | Reciprocity, commitment, social proof, liking, authority, scarcity, unity. Use real instances only |
| **$100M Offers** (Hormozi) | Value = (dream outcome × likelihood) ÷ (time × effort). Raise the top, shrink the bottom. Name the offer, stack bonuses, reverse the risk with a guarantee |
| **The Adweek Copywriting Handbook** (Sugarman) | The first sentence exists to get the second one read. Short, easy, curiosity at each step |
| **The Boron Letters** (Halbert) | The list matters more than the copy: sell to a starving crowd. Write like one person to one person |
| **The Robert Collier Letter Book** | Enter the conversation already in the reader's mind |
| **Cashvertising** (Whitman) | Eight wants people are born with: survival, food, freedom from fear and pain, sex, comfort, winning, protecting loved ones, approval |
| **Building a StoryBrand** (Miller) | The customer is the hero. The brand is the guide. The product is the plan. One clear call to action, and what failure and success look like |
| **Made to Stick** (Heath) | Simple, unexpected, concrete, credible, emotional, story |

## Copy brief (`docs/copy-brief.md`)

Fill this in before writing anything. Every answer cites a source: a review, a support email, a comment, a number.

1. **Who** is the customer, and what group do they belong to?
2. **Desire:** what do they already want? In their own words.
3. **Awareness level** of the traffic on this screen or list.
4. **Sophistication stage** of the market.
5. **Mechanism:** how the product delivers, in one sentence.
6. **Proof:** numbers, reviews, authorities, demonstration.
7. **Offer:** price, bundle, bonus, guarantee, shipping (`ecomm-checkout`).
8. **Objections,** each with its answer.
9. **Voice:** who is speaking (founder, creator, brand) and to whom.

## Formulas

| Formula | Use for |
|---|---|
| **PAS:** problem, agitate, solve | Problem-aware ads and emails |
| **AIDA:** attention, interest, desire, action | Product pages, long emails |
| **Before / after / bridge** | Short ads, subject lines |
| **StoryBrand:** hero, problem, guide, plan, action, stakes | Landing pages, welcome email |
| **Four U's:** useful, urgent, unique, ultra-specific | Checking a headline |

## Writing rules

- One reader, one idea, one call to action.
- Their words, from reviews and comments. Collect them in `docs/voice-of-customer.md`.
- Specific beats general. A number, a name, a time.
- Benefit first, then the feature that causes it.
- Plain words, short sentences. Read it aloud.
- No em dashes, no "unlock", "elevate", "seamless", "game-changer", no three-adjective lists. It reads as a machine and people stop.
- Write three angles for anything that matters (for example: the problem, the proof, the identity) and test them (`ecomm-experiments`).

## Ads and hooks

- Write each hook for one awareness level. Cold traffic gets the problem or a story, never the product name first.
- 3–5 formats: problem then fix, before/after, unboxing, "things I wish I knew", a customer's reaction.
- One landing page per ad angle. The headline on the page repeats the promise in the ad.
- Paid or gifted creators disclose it on every post.

## Where each idea goes

| Screen or message | Lead with |
|---|---|
| Ad to cold traffic | Problem or story (unaware, problem aware) |
| Landing page | The desire and the mechanism, proof early |
| Product page | Specific benefits, mechanism, reviews, the offer, objections answered |
| Cart and checkout | Reassurance only: guarantee, shipping, returns. No new selling |
| Welcome email | The guide's voice, the plan, one action |
| Abandoned cart email | The objection that stopped them, answered |
| Post-purchase | How to get the result. Then the next product |
